E-Learning · Articulate Storyline

Articulate Storyline Sample Course

Interactive Module · Articulate Storyline 360

▶ Launch Course
↑ 16-slide Storyline module with an embedded branching simulation

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Audience

Graduate learners practicing Enhanced Milieu Teaching (EMT): the same clinical skill and learner group behind the Mia simulator, revisited through a standard authoring-tool build.

⁉️ Problem

My earlier EMT prototypes were hand-coded in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. That approach produces exactly the experience I want, but it is not how most instructional design teams work; a build that only I can maintain is a liability on a shared team.

📍 Goal

Rebuild the core of the EMT practice experience natively in Articulate Storyline 360, so the same instructional thinking lives in a tool any team member can open, edit, and extend.

🔑 Approach

Built a 16-slide module in Storyline 360 using layers, triggers, and states, then embedded the branching EMT simulation as a web object so learners move from instruction into practice without leaving the module. Published to HTML5 and self-hosted on GitHub Pages rather than relying on a review-link expiry.

🎯 Outcome

A live, self-hosted Storyline module demonstrating layer and trigger logic, web object integration, and clean HTML5 publishing, built from scratch on a Storyline trial in a single week.

💡 Reflection

Learning Storyline after building the same experience by hand was clarifying. Coding taught me what interaction design actually requires; Storyline taught me how to deliver it in a form a team can own. The constraint of a timeline-and-trigger model made me more deliberate about what genuinely needs to be interactive.

Tools: Articulate Storyline 360 · HTML5 Publishing · Web Objects · GitHub Pages

Simulation · AI · Accessibility

Mia — EMT Practice Simulator

Online Course Conversion · Vanderbilt University SPED 7800

▶ Branching Scenario 🤖 AI Simulator
↑ Two live prototypes for a high-stakes online conversion challenge

⚠️ A friendly note: the AI Simulator runs on a real API — please don't play it so much that you bankrupt me! 😅

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Audience

Graduate students in SPED 7800 at Vanderbilt University, learning Enhanced Milieu Teaching (EMT) — a naturalistic, evidence-based language intervention for children with communication delays.

⁉️ Problem

A core in-person EMT practice assignment — where students interact live with a child — had no clear equivalent for an online audience. The instructional challenge: how do online learners practice a relational, real-time clinical skill without a live child or clinical supervisor in the room?

📍 Goal

Prototype two distinct digital solutions that simulate the EMT practice experience online, enabling the faculty to evaluate which approach — or combination — best supports the course's online conversion.

🔑 Approach

Grounded both prototypes in published EMT research (Kaiser & Pak, in press; Frey & Kaiser, 2011) and the faculty's own course materials. Built a Custom GPT simulator for open-ended, AI-driven practice with debrief, and a hand-coded branching scenario for structured, beginner-friendly interaction — complete with strategy labels, scene-by-scene feedback, and a scored assessment. The branching scenario includes a research-grounded onboarding module and meets WCAG AA accessibility standards.

🎯 Outcome

Two live prototypes presented to the faculty SME for review and feedback. The project established a reusable framework for converting clinical simulation activities to online formats — applicable across health sciences and education courses.

💡 Reflection

This project pushed me to think beyond content delivery into experience design — how to replicate not just the information, but the judgment and responsiveness that clinical practice requires. Building both an AI and a hard-coded version side by side made the tradeoffs between flexibility and structure very concrete.

Tools: HTML · CSS · JavaScript · GitHub Pages · Claude Code · Anthropic API · Cloudflare Workers · WCAG AA Compliance

AI Tool · Higher Education

From Research to Learning Activities

A Gemini AI App for Instructional Designers

🔗 Try the App ▶ Watch Demo
↑ Accelerates design-to-activity workflow

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Audience

Instructional designers working with SMEs across disciplines; faculty designing new or revised courses in online or accelerated formats.

⁉️ Problem

IDs working outside their domain spend disproportionate time decoding dense research, often defaulting to surface-level assessments and relying heavily on SMEs to generate ideas — reinforcing an imbalance where the SME creates and the ID executes.

📍 Goal

Give IDs a structured starting point for activity ideation, accelerate the research-to-design workflow, and enable more sophisticated, scenario-based learning suggestions across unfamiliar disciplines.

🔑 Approach

Built a web application in Gemini AI Studio where users upload a PDF or enter a DOI. The system extracts key ideas and generates pedagogically grounded activity suggestions, mirroring how experienced IDs progress from comprehension to application and transfer.

🎯 Outcome

The tool shifts the ID's role from note-taker to learning strategist — enabling designers to enter SME conversations with structured, application-oriented proposals rather than blank slates, shortening design cycles significantly.

💡 Reflection

Building this app deepened my understanding of AI as a cognitive accelerator. Rather than replacing expertise, it augments pedagogical thinking — freeing IDs to focus on alignment, learner experience, and assessment strategy.

Tools: Gemini AI Studio, Google Cloud Run

E-Learning · Adobe Captivate

Career 101 — Self Discovery Module

Async E-learning Module · Adobe Captivate

🔗 View Module
↑ Positive learner feedback on interactive design

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Audience

College students in New Zealand seeking career guidance, and Guo Ye — a career coach migrating her one-on-one practice to a scalable e-learning model.

⁉️ Problem

Guo Ye's coaching content was unstructured and designed for in-person delivery. She had limited knowledge of the New Zealand career services landscape and no prior e-learning experience.

📍 Goal

Consolidate Guo Ye's coaching philosophy into a structured framework, then transfer it into an accessible, interactive e-learning module for self-directed learners before their coaching sessions.

🔑 Approach

Researched New Zealand's career services landscape and adopted Holland's Theory as the course framework. Conducted interviews with international students to surface their needs, then co-developed a SEPA (Self-reflection, Ethics, Plan, Action) model with the SME and built the module in Adobe Captivate with interactive quizzes and reflection activities.

🎯 Outcome

Draft testers and the client provided positive feedback on the interactive quiz design and SEPA framework structure. The project is ongoing with iterative refinements based on learner testing.

💡 Reflection

Navigating two distinct stakeholders — the coach and the learners — while building the course framework from scratch was a significant learning experience. It sharpened my stakeholder interview skills and deepened my Adobe Captivate proficiency.

Tools: Adobe Captivate, Miro, Amazon Web Services

E-Learning · Faculty Workshop

Empathic Course Design Workshop

Self-Guided Online Workshop · Columbia University SPS

▶ Watch Demo
↑ 25% increase in client follow-up inquiries

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Audience

Faculty members at Columbia University's School of Professional Studies — subject matter experts who often struggle to connect course design with students' lived learning needs.

⁉️ Problem

Despite deep expertise, many SPS faculty found it difficult to design courses that genuinely aligned with students' learning goals and engaged a diverse student body. The gap between expertise and empathic design was real but underacknowledged.

📍 Goal

Raise faculty awareness of empathic course design and equip them with practical, immediately applicable strategies to strengthen cognitive and social empathy in their teaching practice.

🔑 Approach

Interviewed ID colleagues to surface practical insights, then researched Empathic Course Design theory to structure the workshop. Developed an instructional video using CapCut, shared student feedback examples to illustrate the problem, and distributed a curated resource list for ongoing application.

🎯 Outcome

12 faculty members and IDs attended. Three reached out for further discussion post-workshop. Follow-up inquiry rate increased by 25%, and the workshop elevated empathic design as a team-wide conversation among the ID team.

💡 Reflection

Designing this workshop revealed how video production skills are essential for modern instructional designers. Exploring CapCut as a lightweight, accessible tool was a creative constraint that pushed me to focus on clarity and storytelling over production complexity.

Tools: Canva, Canvas LMS, Camtasia, CapCut, Google Docs & Sheets

UX · App Design · SEL

DecideWise Learning App

Educational Mobile App Design — Collaborative Project

▶ Watch Demo
↑ 2nd most sought investment in class showcase

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Audience

Emerging adults aged 18–26 who face pivotal life decisions without adequate structured support for building decision-making skills.

⁉️ Problem

Young adults navigate major decisions — career, relationships, finances — largely without frameworks or skill-building resources. Decision-making is rarely treated as a learnable, teachable skill.

📍 Goal

Raise awareness that decision-making is a learnable skill, and provide emerging adults with a gamified, evidence-based mobile experience to practice and strengthen it.

🔑 Approach

Grounded the app's design in SEL (Social Emotional Learning) research and literature. Incorporated cost-benefit evaluation models, guided introspection activities, and gamification elements. Developed animated pitch videos to explain app functions and used Figma to prototype the user interface collaboratively.

🎯 Outcome

The app emerged as the second most sought-after investment opportunity in the class showcase. Multiple classmates reached out post-presentation to request additional SEL activities and resources.

💡 Reflection

Collaborating across stakeholders while building an entirely new framework from scratch required disciplined prioritization. The project reinforced how stakeholder interviews and literature grounding together create more resonant, credible design rationale.

Tools: Figma, Adobe Premiere Pro, Canva
